A diagnosis is useful when someone is going to decide something with it. Otherwise it ends up as a well-presented report that gets filed away. Before asking for one, it is worth answering three questions.

**Which decision do you want to make?** Opening a new line, reorganising a department, entering another city. The more specific the decision, the more focused the diagnosis.

**Who will make it?** If the decision belongs to management, management has to take part from the start. A diagnosis that arrives from outside without listening to the people who decide has little chance of changing anything.

**What would you do differently depending on the result?** If the answer is "nothing", you do not need the diagnosis yet. If there are two possible paths, the diagnosis helps you choose between them.

With those three answers written down, the work starts with a clear goal, and the result has somewhere to be applied.
