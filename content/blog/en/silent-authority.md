There is a quick way to tell whether someone has implemented artificial intelligence in a real
operation: listen to them for thirty seconds.

Whoever has done it talks about data that was in worse shape than the inventory claimed. About
permissions. About the person on the team who held the whole process in their head and whom nobody
had documented. About the week it had to be switched off and rolled back. They talk about
governance, traceability, and what happens when the system gets it wrong at three in the morning.

Whoever has not talks about revolution.

## Why it happens

It is not bad faith. The two conversations belong to different moments of a project. Enthusiasm
lives in the phase where everything is still possible because nothing has been touched; detail shows
up in month four, when the pilot worked and the rollout is not moving.

The problem for whoever decides is that both sound equally confident. And the one that sounds better
is, without fail, the one that has touched the system least.

## What to do with it

It is a cheap heuristic and it works in a meeting: **ask what went wrong**.

Not "what results did you get?", which has a prepared answer. Ask what they had to undo, what they
underestimated, and what they would do differently. Whoever has been inside answers in thirty
seconds and in detail. Whoever has not changes the subject towards potential.

It is the same discipline as **Destrucción Creativa**: what has to stop explains more than what has
to start. In a sales conversation too.
